We read public records and publish the numbers.

No paywall, no private data, no black box. Every figure we publish can be traced back to the file it came out of and the day we pulled it.

How a report gets made

  1. We pick a public file that a federal agency already publishes — injury and inspection records, awards, filings.
  2. We clean it and count it. Where the file is wrong or incomplete, we say so on the page.
  3. We publish a page with inline charts, readable tables and the CSV behind them.
  4. It goes out on the site of the industry the file belongs to, credited to that site's research desk, with Strata Data LLC as the operator.

One example of the method: a ten-year analysis of federal severe-injury reports, published as charts, tables and downloadable CSV, with the extraction method written out so a reader can check it.

What we publish

  • Charts. Drawn inline, so they load as page content and stay readable in an archive.
  • Tables. The counts behind every chart, not just the summary line.
  • CSV. The same numbers as a file you can open in a spreadsheet.
  • Method notes. Which file, which fields, which filter, which day.

Republishing

Charts, tables and CSV from our report pages may be republished free of charge with attribution. One line is enough: name the site and the file, for example "Source: analysis of federal OSHA severe injury reports".

Want a cut that is not on the page — a state, an industry code, a year, a category? Ask. Small cuts we usually answer for free; a large one we scope first. tony@dilisights.com.

What we will not do

We do not publish anything that identifies an injured person, a patient, a minor or a private individual. We do not use private or leaked data. We do not sell a ranking or a placement. We do not publish a number without naming its source, and we date every pull, because agencies revise their files.
